Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, *"SF1-lineage identity, not the highest-risk k4 cluster, defines non-functioning pituitary neuroendocrine tumors: independent cross-cohort and cross-platform methylation validation,"* for consideration as an original article in *Neuro-Oncology*.

**Relevance to Neuro-Oncology.** Non-functioning pituitary neuroendocrine tumors (NF-PitNETs) are the most common clinically silent pituitary tumors and a routine part of neuro-oncology practice, yet their biological classification and post-surgical regrowth risk remain poorly predicted by histopathology alone. A recently proposed five-cluster DNA-methylation taxonomy stratified NF-PitNETs into risk subgroups, but its generalizability to independent cohorts was untested, and its highest-risk cluster (k4) is increasingly conflated with "aggressive NF-PitNET" in the literature.

**What we did.** We independently reanalyzed four public cohorts — three EPIC-850K (GSE283928, GSE207937, E-MTAB-7762/Neou) and one HM450 (GSE115783) — using from-scratch joint PCA, nearest-centroid projection, and the 562 published cluster-specific differentially methylated probes, together with a direct prognostic test in Møller et al.'s own 117-NF-PitNET cohort using reoperation as a hard endpoint.

**Main findings.**
1. NF-PitNETs reproducibly map to a single SF1-lineage methylation identity (cluster c0) across all four cohorts and both platforms (mean 84.3%; range 75.6–94.1%).
2. This lineage identity is **distinct from the highest-risk k4 cluster** (TPIT/PIT1), which maps instead to the secretory clusters c3/c4 (probe-level p = 1.6×10⁻³⁰) — correcting a misinterpretation that is gaining traction in the field.
3. The within-lineage k-subdivision is independently recoverable from public data (99.1% leave-one-out), and direct prognosis in Møller's cohort suggests the high-risk axis (k3/k4/k5) trends toward increased regrowth (HR 2.14, p = 0.072; adjusted HR 2.09, p = 0.088) — reported throughout as suggestive/borderline, with explicit power and surrogate-endpoint caveats.

**Significance.** To our knowledge this is the first independent, cross-cohort and cross-platform validation of the Møller taxonomy. It reframes NF-PitNET risk stratification from lineage status per se to a within-lineage subdivision, and nominates lineage-aware therapeutic candidates (HDAC, mTOR, BET inhibition) for future preclinical validation.
